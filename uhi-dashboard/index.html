Methodology

Sequential spatial mining pipeline applied to multi-year Sentinel-2 and Landsat imagery over Chennai.

🛰️
Data Acquisition

Sentinel-2 SR (NDVI, NDBI) + Landsat-8/9 (LST) from Google Earth Engine, 2016–2024. Annual median composites per ward.

→
🗺️
Spatial Aggregation

200 actual Chennai ward polygons via GeoJSON. Mean values extracted per ward using reduceRegions in GEE.

→
📈
Mann-Kendall Trend

Non-parametric monotonic trend test (pymannkendall) per ward LST series. Identifies significant warming/cooling at p < 0.05.

→
🔍
PELT Change-Point

Pruned Exact Linear Time (ruptures) detects structural breaks in the LST series — sudden shifts from events like new development zones.

→
📊
OLS Regression

LST ~ NDVI + NDBI + rainfall across all ward-years. R² = 0.17 at ward scale, revealing scale-dependence of vegetation–heat relationship.

🔬 Key Finding

NDBI (built-up index) has a stronger positive effect on LST (β = 23.5) than NDVI has a cooling effect (β = 5.7), suggesting that urban expansion outpaces vegetation's mitigation capacity in Chennai.

⚠️ Limitations

9-year series is short for robust trend detection. Ward-mean averaging smooths sub-ward hotspots. 2015 excluded — Sentinel-2 coverage over India was sparse before 2016.

🧰 Tools Used

Python · Google Earth Engine · GeoPandas · pymannkendall · ruptures · scikit-learn · folium · Leaflet.js · Chart.js
